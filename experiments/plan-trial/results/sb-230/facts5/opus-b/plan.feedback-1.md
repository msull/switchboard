1. **The reissue runs only at bind, but the QR reads the address at draw time, so the SAN check covers an address the QR may no longer show.**
   - What the decision rests on. Plan lines 128–143 reissue "when a name from `certificate_names()` is missing", and only inside `SwitchboardApp::bind_remote`. The tree runs `bind_remote` only at `listen_remote` (startup) and when `identity.listen` changes (`src/app.rs:2894-2898`, `:2936`). The QR calls `remote::local_address()` on every frame (`src/ui/remote.rs:156-158`). On a 0.0.0.0 bind, an address change never rebinds: think of a laptop that moves to another Wi-Fi, renews DHCP, or brings a VPN up, which per Facts, Settled, line 9 moves `local_address()`. After that, the QR prints an IP that is not in the SAN.
   - Why that matters. The plan reissues because a name missing from the SAN may make Safari and Chrome refuse with no click-through (Facts, Unresolved, line 27). So as written, the "current address is in the SAN" property holds only if the address does not change between bind and scan. That is a favourable answer to an unresolved question, and the plan does not say it relies on it. The 30-day renewal has the same flaw: an app that runs across the renewal point keeps the old certificate until expiry. That contradicts line 27's build-to, "with renewal before expiry".
   - Fix. Run the `reissued` check, and rebind when it yields a certificate, at the moment the pairing window opens (`AppAction::OpenPairing` in the app layer, before the QR is drawn). Also run it on the existing once-a-second poll, or on the `Tick` for expiry. Then the address the QR shows is one the certificate carries. Alternatively, build the QR from the SAN's address rather than from a fresh `local_address()`, and have the dialog say when they differ. Either way, add a test: change the fake's address and open pairing, and a new certificate whose SAN holds the new address is served.

2. **The token's whole home is a cookie that a browser must store and send after a certificate click-through. That mechanic is in neither the facts nor the tree, and the plan does not make it the implementer's first test.**
   - Whether the store is right. The plan picks the safer store, an `HttpOnly; Secure; SameSite=Strict` `__Host-` cookie (plan lines 147–148, 206–210). The issue offered that option (issue #230, decision 2). The choice is right.
   - What is unresolved. Its mechanics are not settled. Facts line 12 settles secure context only for `isSecureContext` / `crypto.randomUUID`. Facts line 13 settles that the upgrade *carries* cookies. Nothing settles that Safari or Chrome *accept* a `Secure` `Set-Cookie` from a response on an origin whose certificate was clicked through. Nothing settles that they send it on the `wss` upgrade either.
   - What I could not settle. Queries run:
     - "Chrome cookies not saved certificate error page proceed anyway self-signed Secure cookie stored";
     - "Safari iOS Set-Cookie Secure HttpOnly self-signed certificate after "visit this website" cookie not stored";
     - "chromium "certificate error" cookies Set-Cookie ignored when SSL error bypassed URLRequestHttpJob".

     None gives a vendor answer. WebKit bug 232088 (https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=232088) shows WebKit rejecting `Secure` cookies where it judges the context not secure, while Chromium and Gecko accept them. That is not this case, but it shows that WebKit's cookie "secure" test is not the same as the scheme test in Facts line 12.
   - Fix. Name this line as the implementer's first test, on the owner's iPhone and before any page code: "after accepting the warning, `/pair` with `cookie: true`, then `GET device` with the page header, returns 200". Add it to "For the owner" beside the `wss` check. State the fallback in the plan for a "no". Every authenticated call then fails, and polling does not rescue it as it rescues the socket.

1. **Step 2 (a certificate re-made in memory) assumes, with no support, that the browser's click-through outlives a new certificate. It doesn't, so the plan makes U2's unfavourable branch certain without taking it.**

   The plan makes the SAN certificate "in memory" and "re-makes the certificate over the same `rcgen::KeyPair` when the address moved". Its stated reason is to keep `identity.json` and `IDENTITY_SCHEMA_VERSION` unchanged (plan, "The record"). It also assumes, without saying so, that a re-made certificate is no event for a browser. Nothing in the facts or the tree supports that:
   - The key is P-256 (`src/adapters/remote.rs:90`, `rcgen::KeyPair::generate()` at `:101`). The provider is `ring` (`:160-162`), whose ECDSA signing uses a random nonce. The plan also sets a fresh 397-day validity. So every launch, and every address move, serves a certificate with different DER, even though the key and `spki_sha256` stay the same.
   - Chrome keys a click-through decision on the certificate, not the host. The `SSLHostStateDelegate` header says `AllowCert` "Records that |cert| is permitted to be used for |host| in the future, for a specified |error| type", and `QueryPolicy` asks "whether |cert| is allowed for |host| and |error|": https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/main/content/public/browser/ssl_host_state_delegate.h . Firefox stores the accepted certificate's fingerprint against host:port. That is indicative only: Mozilla bug 1109773 comment, https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1109773 , and https://community.ipfire.org/t/firefox-suddenly-rejecting-webui-certificate/11722 . I found no Safari source either way. Queries run: "Firefox certificate exception override keyed by certificate fingerprint host port new certificate warning again" and "Chrome SSLHostStateDelegate certificate error decision remembered per host and certificate".

   As planned, every browser shows the warning again after each app launch and each change of address. It does so before the poll can reach the host, so a paired page shows "unreachable or not trusted" until the owner clicks through again. The facts' U2 line says what that outcome requires: "Unfavourable: the pairing window's text names the warning as repeating." The plan's help text says the browser "will warn about the certificate". That reads as a one-time warning, and the design.md gaps list doesn't mention it. A warning on every launch also trains the owner to click through, which hides the one signal a browser has of an impostor: a warning when none was expected.

   Pick one:
   - (a) Persist the browser certificate, keyed by the address set it covers, so it changes only when the address does. It could live in a new file beside `identity.json` in the data directory, written through the store's atomic path. Or it could be a field on `Identity` with `IDENTITY_SCHEMA_VERSION` 1 -> 2 and a migrate step. In both cases `spki_sha256` and the native pins are unchanged.
   - (b) Keep the certificate in memory, and say plainly in the pairing window's line, in §5 and in design.md's gaps that the warning returns after every launch and every change of address.

   Either way, the step 1 spike should record whether a re-issued certificate (same key) brings the warning back in Safari and in Chrome. That is the only browser this point isn't settled for.
